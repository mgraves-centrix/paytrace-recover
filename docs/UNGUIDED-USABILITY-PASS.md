# Unguided usability pass

Use the deployed sandbox demo with fictional records. Give each participant only the URL and the task below; do not explain the navigation or the intended answer. Record time, wrong turns, questions, and the participant's own words. Do not use a real buyer, card, password, or production purchase.

## Merchant: find the next case

Start at `/focus.html`. Ask: “A customer paid but cannot open the digital course. Find the case that needs your attention, explain what PayTrace knows, and say what you would do next.”

Success: the participant finds the case from Today or the queue, distinguishes a sample from a PayPal sandbox capture, identifies the missing access evidence, and can name the next safe action without prompting. Note whether the case page's first screen makes that action visible before they open advanced evidence.

## Merchant: approve safely

Use a fictional eligible case with current evidence and a prepared correction preview. Ask: “Decide whether you would approve this access change. Show which purchase and learner it affects, what changes, and how you would know it worked.”

Success: the participant identifies the exact purchase and learner, recognizes that approval is separate from execution and verification, and can find the customer communication area. A sample, mismatched, stale, or unavailable source must not look safe to approve. Stop before any write unless the operator explicitly chose this sandbox exercise.

## Buyer: understand progress

Give the participant a fictional private case link. Ask: “Find out what happened to your purchase, send a question, and check whether the lesson opens.”

Success: the participant finds the current status and next update, knows how to message the merchant, understands that a technical check and browser receipt differ from their own confirmation, and does not think they must pay again.

## Record and decide

For each task, record completion (yes/no), elapsed time, first point of confusion, any misleading claim, and whether help was needed. Treat a mistaken approval, a mistaken “delivered” interpretation, or inability to find customer messages as a release-blocking UX issue. Rework the specific label or layout, then repeat with a new participant. This checklist is a test plan, not evidence that an unguided test has already passed.
